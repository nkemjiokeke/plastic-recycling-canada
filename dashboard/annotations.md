# Datawrapper annotations - one per chart, tied to a recommendation or issue

Drop these onto the chart canvas in Datawrapper (Annotate tab). Keep the source line in the footer of every chart:
Source: Statistics Canada Table 38-10-0138 and 17-10-0009; Environment and Climate Change Canada.

## 1. Choropleth - plastic diverted per person (map_datawrapper_per_capita.csv)
- On Ontario: "Ontario diverts the most plastic in total but only 7.9 kg per person, below several smaller provinces. Its large population and low per-person rate make it the single biggest opportunity to move the national number."
- On Nova Scotia: "Nova Scotia leads at 14.5 kg per person. A smaller province can outperform, so scale is not the constraint."
- Issue/rec link: focus improvement where the base is large but the rate is low (Ontario).

## 2. Choropleth - total plastic diverted (chart_concentration_2022.csv)
- Central annotation: "Ontario, Quebec and British Columbia hold 79% of all plastic diverted. National progress depends mostly on programs in these three provinces."
- Rec link: concentrate national effort and EPR support where the volume is.

## 3. Line - national trend and projection to 2030 (dw_trend_projection.csv)
- On the actual line: "Plastic diverted rose less than 3% between 2018 and 2022 - effectively flat."
- On the projected line: "At the current pace, Canada reaches about 389,000 tonnes by 2030, far short of a zero-waste target. Meeting it requires a step change, not the current trajectory."
- Issue link: the 2030 target is not achievable on trend; measurement and targets need to change.

## 4. Arrow / range plot - change by province 2018 to 2022 (dw_change_2018_2022.csv)
- Header note: "The flat national line hides large provincial swings."
- On Saskatchewan: "Saskatchewan fell 37%, the steepest drop. Worth investigating for a lost program or a reporting gap."
- On Quebec and Newfoundland: "Quebec (+26%) and Newfoundland (+45%) grew fastest. Their recent changes may be worth studying and copying."
- Rec link: learn from the risers, diagnose the fallers.

## 5. Stacked bar - residential vs business by province (dw_source_by_province_2022.csv)
- Central annotation: "In every province, households provide most of the diverted plastic. The business and institutional stream is the largest untapped source."
- On Saskatchewan (about 94% residential): "Saskatchewan's diverted plastic is almost entirely residential; its commercial plastic is barely collected."
- Rec link: extended producer responsibility and commercial collection should target the non-residential stream.

## 6. Table - province scorecard (dw_summary_table.csv)
- Intro note above the table: "Sort by any column. Provinces with large totals but low per-person rates (Ontario) are the biggest opportunities; provinces high on both (Nova Scotia) show what is achievable."
- Formatting: round per-person to one decimal; New Brunswick residential share is blank because that cell is suppressed at source.

## 7. Scatter - total-waste rate vs plastic per person, 2020 (dw_rate_vs_percapita_2020.csv)
- On PEI: "PEI diverts the most total waste per person but only mid-table on plastic. A strong overall system does not automatically capture plastic."
- On Newfoundland: "Newfoundland is lowest on both measures, the clearest place to build basic capacity."
- Issue link: plastic needs its own attention, separate from general waste performance.

## 8. Donut - national plastic fate (canada_plastic_fate_2016_ECCC.csv)
- Central annotation: "Only 9% of Canada's plastic is recycled; 86% is landfilled. This is the roughly $8 billion of material Canada discards every year."
- Issue link: ties the whole piece back to the economic loss and the 2030 commitment.
