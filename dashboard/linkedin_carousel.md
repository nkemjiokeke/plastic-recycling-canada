## POST CAPTION
Canada has committed to zero plastic waste by 2030. Using Statistics Canada and Environment and Climate Change Canada data, I checked whether that is on track. The data says it is not.

About 9% of Canada's plastic is recycled, the amount diverted has been flat since 2018, and roughly $8 billion of plastic material is discarded each year.

The more useful part is the provincial breakdown: which provinces divert the most, whether it comes from households or businesses, and where the biggest opportunity sits.

The findings are in the slides. Sources and method are in the comments.

## SLIDE 2 - The gap  [chart: national fate donut]
Headline: Only 9% is recycled.
Body: 86% is landfilled, 4% is burned, 1% is lost. The plastic Canada does not recover is worth about $8 billion a year.

## SLIDE 3 - Not on track  [chart: national trend + projection]
Headline: Diversion has been flat since 2018.
Body: Plastic diverted rose less than 3% in four years. On the current pace Canada reaches about 389,000 tonnes by 2030 - far short of a zero-waste target.

## SLIDE 4 - Where the volume is  [chart: totals map or ranked bar]
Headline: Three provinces hold 79%.
Body: Ontario (33%), Quebec (29%) and British Columbia divert most of Canada's plastic. National progress depends on programs in these three.

## SLIDE 5 - The per-person flip  [chart: per-capita choropleth]
Headline: The biggest recycler is average per person.
Body: Nova Scotia diverts 14.5 kg per person. Ontario, the largest in total, sits 5th at 7.9 kg. Size is not the constraint.

## SLIDE 6 - Total vs per person  [chart: scatter, tonnes vs kg per person]
Headline: Big in total does not mean big per person.
Body: Ontario is far right (largest total) but low down; Nova Scotia is high but small. This is where the real performance gap shows.

## SLIDE 7 - Flat hides movement  [chart: arrow / range plot]
Headline: The national line hides big provincial swings.
Body: Saskatchewan fell 37%. Quebec rose 26% and Newfoundland 45%. Some provinces are moving fast - in both directions.

## SLIDE 8 - Households carry it  [chart: residential vs business stacked bar]
Headline: 71% comes from household bins.
Body: Only 27% of diverted plastic comes from businesses and institutions, where a large share of plastic is actually used. The commercial stream is the biggest untapped source.

## SLIDE 9 - Good overall is not good at plastic  [chart: rate vs per-capita scatter]
Headline: Diverting waste well does not mean diverting plastic well.
Body: PEI leads on total waste but is only mid-table on plastic. Newfoundland is lowest on both. Plastic needs its own attention.

## SLIDE 10 - The scorecard  [chart: sortable table]
Headline: Every province, side by side.
Body: Total diverted, per person, residential share and overall diversion rate. High total but low per person (Ontario) is the biggest opportunity.

## SLIDE 11 - What would move the number
Headline: Three ways to make progress.
Body:
1. Collect business and industrial plastic, not just household bins.
2. Focus on Ontario, Quebec and British Columbia, where the volume is.
3. Set province-level targets and measure them - the Auditor General found the current data cannot track progress.

## SLIDE 12 - Close
Headline: The target is 2030. The trend says we will miss it.
Body: But the data also shows exactly where to act. All figures from Statistics Canada and Environment and Climate Change Canada.
